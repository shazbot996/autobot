## About a glossary

A glossary fixes what this project's words mean. It is **prescriptive**: when the code or a document
uses a term differently, the usage is wrong and the glossary is right, or the glossary is amended on
purpose.

**Each entry is a term and its meaning here**, not its dictionary meaning. If "record" means a pair
of markdown files written once per run, the entry says that, and says what it does not mean ("not a
database row, not a log line"). The negative half is often the useful half, because it is what stops
the word drifting.

**It belongs here:** terms a newcomer would misread, terms the project uses in a narrower sense than
usual, and pairs of terms that are easy to confuse (Refine and Revise; a square and a slot). Where a
term has a history, one line saying what it replaced.

**It does not belong here:** every noun in the codebase, definitions a reader already has, or
explanations of how something works. A glossary entry says what a word means, and stops.

**The failure to avoid** is circularity: a term defined by a word that is itself only defined by the
term. Every entry should be understandable by someone who has read no other entry.
